How to Back Up WhatsApp Chats Safely Before Switching Apps

Switching apps sounds simple until you realise how much sits inside your chat history. Family photos, voice notes, work details and years of conversations are easy to take for granted until an uninstall or a failed restore makes them disappear. A careful backup cannot remove every risk, but it gives you a real chance of keeping what matters.

This guide covers the options Android users have before switching: Google Drive backup, local backup, exported chats, and separate media copies. It also explains honestly what a backup cannot promise, especially when moving between the official app and a third-party app.

Why a Backup Matters Before You Switch

Uninstalling an app, clearing its data or signing in on a new device can remove the local copy of your chats, and without a recent backup there may be nothing to restore from. A backup also protects against an interrupted download, low storage, or an app failing after an update.

Before You Start: A Quick Checklist

  • Use reliable Wi-Fi if your backup is large.
  • Keep your phone charging during the backup.
  • Check free space on your phone and Google account.
  • Note your Google account and WhatsApp phone number.
  • Do not uninstall WhatsApp until the backup is verified.

Option One: Google Drive Backup in WhatsApp Settings

For most Android users this is the simplest starting point. It saves your chat history and, if you choose, your media to your Google account, and can be offered when you set up WhatsApp again with the same number and account.

How to Run a Google Drive Backup

Open WhatsApp and go to Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup. That screen shows when the last local and Google Drive backups were made, and their size. Tap Back up now, keep WhatsApp open on Wi-Fi, and wait until the date and time update to now.

Check the Google account shown before backing up: a backup saved to the wrong account will be useless later. For a planned switch, always run a manual backup on the day, because an automatic schedule may not have run recently.

Include Videos Only If You Need Them

Videos make backups much larger and slower. If they are saved elsewhere, leaving the include-videos option off makes the backup faster. If they matter and you have no other copy, turn it on and allow extra time.

Turn On End-to-End Encrypted Backup

The Chat backup screen offers an end-to-end encrypted backup option, protected by a password you create or a 64-digit key WhatsApp generates, so nobody is meant to read it without that password or key. If you forget the password and lose the key, you may not be able to restore that backup at all. Store it safe, separate from the phone, never only inside a WhatsApp chat.

Option Two: Local Backup on Android

WhatsApp also creates local backups on your phone, usually overnight. They do not depend on your Google account, but live on the same device, so a lost, damaged or reset phone can take them with it. Copy them somewhere else.

Where Local Backups Live

On many Android phones the files sit in the WhatsApp folder, in a subfolder called Databases, with names based on msgstore and a date. The wider folder can also hold a Media folder with photos, videos and documents. Locations vary, so look carefully and do not delete anything you do not recognise.

Copying the WhatsApp Folder and Databases

Using a file manager, or by connecting your phone to a computer by USB, copy the whole WhatsApp folder to a computer, external drive or separate cloud account. Copying the whole folder, rather than one database file, keeps chats and media together. Compare its size with the original and leave the original untouched.

Option Three: Export Individual Chats

An export is not a full backup and cannot be restored as a working chat history. It is, however, an excellent safety net: a readable copy of an important conversation that survives even if a full restore never works.

Open the conversation, tap the menu, choose More, then Export chat, and save or email the result. Export chats you cannot recreate, such as business arrangements or family records, even if you have already made a full backup.

Back Up Your Media Separately

Photos and videos deserve their own copy. A chat restore can leave media missing if files were deleted, excluded from the backup, or stored in a folder the new app does not read. Copy the WhatsApp Images, Video, Voice Notes and Documents folders to the same safe destination.

  • Copy media to a computer or drive, not only to the same phone.
  • Open a few copied photos and videos to check they work.
  • Keep voice notes and documents, which are easy to overlook.
  • If you use a photo backup service, confirm WhatsApp folders are included.

Verify Your Backup Before You Uninstall Anything

This is the step people skip, and it is the most important one. An unchecked backup is only an assumption. Return to Settings, Chats, then Chat backup, and read what the screen says.

  • Confirm the last backup date and time are from today.
  • Check the backup size looks realistic for your history.
  • Confirm the Google account listed is the right one.
  • Open your copied folder elsewhere and check the files.
  • Check you still have your encrypted backup password or key.

If the date is old, the size looks too small or the copy is missing, run the backup again while WhatsApp is still working. Do not uninstall first and investigate afterwards.

Keep Two Copies, in Two Places

Two copies in two places are much stronger than one: for example, a Google Drive backup plus a copied WhatsApp folder on a computer. Label each copy with its date, and keep them until the new app works normally and your chats and media are confirmed present.

What Can Go Wrong When Restoring Into a Different App

A Safer Order for Switching

  • Run a Google Drive backup and let it finish.
  • Copy the WhatsApp folder, including Databases and Media, to a computer.
  • Export your most important individual chats.
  • Verify the backup date, size, account and copied files.
  • Store your encrypted backup password or key safely.
  • Only then set up the other app, keeping backups untouched.
  • After setup, check chats and media before deleting any backup.

If anything fails during setup, stop. Repeatedly uninstalling or overwriting folders can destroy the copies you need. Go back to your saved files and decide calmly whether to restore to the official app instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a WhatsApp backup take?

It depends on your chat and media size, connection and free storage. A small backup on fast Wi-Fi may take minutes; a large one with videos can take much longer. Do not interrupt it.

Will my backup definitely restore into another app?

No. A backup may not restore cleanly into a different app, especially between the official app and an unofficial third-party mod. Make Google Drive, local and exported copies, verify them, and accept that a restore is never promised.

What happens if I forget my encrypted backup password?

If you lose both the password and the 64-digit key for an encrypted backup, you may be unable to restore it. Store them safely, away from the phone, before you switch. Without them, your other copies may be your only option.

Should I back up media separately from my chats?

Yes. A separate copy protects photos, videos, voice notes and documents if a chat restore is incomplete or a folder is not read by the new app. Check a few copied files open properly before uninstalling anything.
